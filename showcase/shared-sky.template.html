Measured results · 95 % ranges

Fewer meetings, and safer when something goes wrong

Each bar is a registered prediction tested on paired runs; the line shows the 95 % range. Sites 3 and 4 km apart, three seeds each.

Shared flight-plan check. If companies also check each other's filed plans before take-off, planned meetings drop to zero (at most about 4 in six runs at 95 % confidence). The cost is 1.5–4.8 % of flights waiting about 40 seconds. The smallest fleet, Amazon, waits most often, mainly because it meets two larger fleets.

What a meeting is. Two planned flights of different companies within 30 m horizontally and 10 m vertically at the same moment, checked four times a second. It is a planning measure, not a collision count.

What we know and what we don't

Scorecard

Measured and robust

  • The 4 m clash comes straight from the filings.
  • The allocation cuts meetings 81 % and close meetings 92 %, whether Wing flies a fixed height or a random one.
  • How meetings grow with traffic matches an independent simulator (BlueSky).

Already published elsewhere

  • Delay grows with the size of the reserved airspace (NASA, 2023).
  • Plan checks by departure delay, and their limits under heavy demand (Airbus UTM and EUROCONTROL).
  • Off-plan aircraft add risk after plans are shared (NASA, 2024), studied with all aircraft at one height.

Not checked against reality

  • Delays between companies: none are published. Wing and Flytrex report about 8,000 shared operations with zero planned conflicts, consistent in kind.
  • Incident rates and return-home heights are assumed.

Known gaps

  • Overflights per home: near Amazon's Richardson hub a resident counted about half of all hub traffic over one house; the model gives about 2.5 %, or 10 % with the hospital no-fly zone.
  • Planned tracks only: no wind, no in-flight avoidance, flat demand through the day.
