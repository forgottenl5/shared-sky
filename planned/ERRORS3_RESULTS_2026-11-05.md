# ERRORS3 results (5 Nov 2026; registration planned/ERRORS3_PREREG.md)
Full tables: planned/errors3_results_table.md. 80 runs (2 metros x 2 arms x no check / V2 x seeds 1-10), 194,205 encounters, 90 m sideways search; checks K1-K3 passed. No deviations.

**3 of 4 predictions passed. Bad GPS downtown barely matters for metro-wide modelled risk, because almost no close encounters happen downtown. The prediction that urban-canyon error everywhere would raise the residual risk with the plan check by at least 50 % failed: the change was -11 % to +17 %.**

## Predictions
- **G1 PASS** urban-canyon sideways error downtown only changes metro-wide risk (no check, L2) by +0.1 % in all four cases.
- **G2 PASS** downtown share of encounters within 30 m: Houston 0.33 % (S1), 0.32 % (A2); DFW 0.14 %, 0.10 %.
- **G3 PASS** urban-canyon sideways error everywhere, no check: Houston +4 % (S1), +5 % (A2); DFW -6 %, +0 %.
- **G4 FAIL** with V2 and canyon error everywhere (lower bound): Houston S1 +13 %, A2 +7 %; DFW S1 +17 %, A2 -11 %. I predicted at least +50 %.

## About G4 (not tested; a hypothesis only)
- RISK2 found that most residual risk under V2 comes from pairs planned 20-25 m apart in height, which the check cannot see. Sideways error does not change that height gap, which would explain why larger sideways error adds little. I have not run a test of this.
- Residual rates rest on few encounters (about 0.5-1.1 per 100,000 flight hours); the -11 % in DFW A2 is within noise, with no interval computed.
- Drifting errors were settled by argument, not runs (see the registration); not scored.

## Reading
- Downtown is 0.19-0.26 % of the counted areas and holds under 0.4 % of close encounters, so poor GPS there hardly moves the metro-wide figure.
- If every place had urban-canyon sideways error, no-check risk changes by at most 6 %: for crossing traffic the expected overlap barely depends on sideways error size.

## Limits
- Downtown is a box, not a building-by-building signal model; the canyon figure (47.8 m raw, 4.4 m processed, 95th percentile) is from one city and one study (NASA Langley MAPT, Columbus); height error assumed unaffected; errors independent between aircraft; still air; no avoidance. With canyon error everywhere, encounters planned more than 90 m apart are not counted, so that case is a lower bound. A model quantity, not a safety assessment.
