# Related-work check for the drone engine's coordination findings (27 Oct 2026)
Method: web searches plus reading of the pages below (abstract-level; a fetch tool summarised them, I did not read full papers). "Not found" means not found in these searches, not "does not exist".

## What exists
- **Airbus/EUROCONTROL, DASC 2023 (Riga).** Hub-and-spoke delivery, 100/200/500 ops/h, one altitude (115 m), strategic deconfliction by departure delay only. Strategic deconfliction cut mid-air-collision rate ~99 %; throughput fell and delays reached 2.5-4.5 h at 500 ops/h (authors call that unrealistic). Synthetic demand; no layers; no company structure.
- **NASA UTM TCL4 report (TM-20205006337, Rios et al., 2020).** Flight test + simulated aircraft; ~95 % of operations deconflicted or covered by negotiation; delays and rejected-plan counts not reported.
- **Doole et al., TU Delft, Aerospace 2021 (Manhattan).** Layered airspace designs (40 layers, 25 ft apart), BlueSky; no strategic delay costs, synthetic demand.
- **Flytrex+Wing (Jun 2026 press).** ~8,000 overlapping flights, 100 % of intents deconflicted; no delay, cancellation or buffer data published.
- **Melgosa et al. (UPC Barcelona), Drones 2026, 10(9):656 (read pp. 1-12 of 36: abstract, introduction, literature review, method; results and discussion not read).** Closest in spirit: multiple competing operators (each distribution centre is a separate company) sharing one city's very-low-level airspace; compares structured airspace (grid corridors, discrete layers, A* routing) with free-route (and layered free-route), 3,500 daily operations in a large European city, demand synthetic and proportional to population density, ground-aware altitudes. Reported (abstract): the grid produces more conflicts than free route (316 vs 54); only the free-route case is fully solved by a simple strategic altitude reassignment (about 10 % unresolved in the grid case). Differences from ours: European city not US filed layouts; synthetic demand not the companies' filings; the output is conflict counts and flight time, not delay or cancellation shares by company; no volume (reservation) model, so no layer-gap versus reservation effect; no wind or forecast-error conformance. Altitude reassignment is related to our height sharing, so I should cite it and not call height sharing new.
- Also exist (not claim-relevant): confidence-based buffers for strategic deconfliction (Wayne State/AIAA), fairness in decentralized strategic deconfliction (Airbus, AIAA 2020).

## What I did not find
1. Delay/cancellation share **by company** for multi-operator delivery coordination built from the **companies' own filed layouts** (Houston, DFW).
2. The specific COORD1/COORD2 result: layer gaps smaller than the sum of the two flights' vertical reservations make neighbouring layers conflict as if shared; sizing gaps to >= 2 x reservation cut delays by ~60 % in the model.
3. Conformance (how often flights leave their own reserved windows in p90 wind, plans made on a persistence forecast).

## Honest status against the portfolio bar
1. **Finding not published elsewhere:** plausible but unproven, and narrow (items 1-3 above are in a behaviour model with assumed volume sizes). The point itself (reservation larger than layer gap) is close to obvious once stated; the value is the numbers for real filed layouts, which a reader must be able to reproduce.
2. **Outside validation anchor:** still missing. Flytrex/Wing is qualitative and consistent by construction. Riga's delay figures are a different model (single altitude, hub-and-spoke), so a comparison would be a cross-check of trend (delay rises steeply with density), not of numbers.
3. **Public reproducible page:** the Lab page exists; COORD1/2 are not on it yet.

## Suggested next steps (cheapest first)
a. Read the rest of Drones 10(9):656 (results, discussion, pp. 13-36) and the NASA/Airbus items in full.
b. Add a density sweep (scale demand 0.25x-2x) so the delay curve can be compared in shape with Riga's.
c. Put COORD1/2 on the Lab page with the code and data, and publish the failed predictions.

## Gemini literature search (founder-supplied, 27 Oct 2026), checked claim by claim
Checked by web search plus fetching the source where the allowlist allowed it. Gemini's interpretive sentences ("rises exponentially", "cannot rely on launch metering", etc.) are not from the sources and are ignored.
- **Pradeep, Munishkin, Kalyanam, Erzberger (NASA Ames), AIAA SciTech 2023, "Strategic Deconfliction of Small Unmanned Aircraft Using Operational Volume Blocks at Crossing Waypoints" (NTRS 20220018218). VERIFIED, and the most relevant item.** Two operators, one depot each, flights crossing at one waypoint; volume blocks sized by length and width; Poisson demand; ground delay. Abstract: crossing-waypoint separation has negligible effect on average ground delay when departure separation at the depot is at least twice the minimum temporal separation. Gemini got the author list wrong. **Consequence:** "volume size drives ground delay between two operators" is published (analytically, one crossing). Our contribution must be stated as the network-scale, filed-layout, multi-company, wind version, and must cite this.
- **Jessen (MIT Lincoln Laboratory), arXiv 2509.08124, "UTM Performance Under Stressing Scenarios". VERIFIED in substance.** F3548-style two-USS lab test, 40 replanned operations; "At baseline, the operation serviced by USS 1 is always rejected" (a race condition between USSs); SCD durations approached 30 s at 40 operations in 30 s. It is about network latency, not airspace capacity; not overlapping with ours.
- **arXiv 2608.23166 (SESAR SIDs 2025), "Leveraging UAV Autonomy for Minimum 4D Flight Authorization Volumes". VERIFIED number, WRONG title in Gemini** (Gemini gave the title of a different, UAM paper, arXiv 2301.12961). 95 % probabilistic envelopes; 0.08 % of trajectory samples outside the reserved volume; Gaussian noise, wind not modelled. Contrast with our 14-15 % of flights leaving their windows in p90 wind with a persistence forecast: worth stating, the gap is the wind and forecast error.
- **Liu, Ramos Gonzalez, Munoz-Gamarra et al. (UAB). PARTLY WRONG.** The IEEE T-ITS paper and DOI Gemini cites could not be found. A related, verified paper by the same group, Transportation Research Part C vol. 185 (2026) 105553 (multi-agent negotiation, four operators), reports FCFS 41.5 % vs batch 49.8 % acceptance; Gemini's 61 % / 35 % and the 30/40/70/80 m layers are not in it. Treat the T-ITS claims as unverified.
- **Rule-based conflict management (SciSpace link), SESAR DACUS SIDs 2021 paper 54, CAELUS ACP, SORA 2.5 Kenya paper:** not checked; none is a delay-vs-density or layer-sizing result for multi-operator delivery.
- **Texas FAA filings section:** descriptions of the environmental assessments the engine already uses, not studies of them. Gemini found no study that uses the filed Texas layouts; neither did I. Its Zipline five-metro numbers (30-65 Chargers and 30,000-65,000 deliveries/day per metro, draft EA Sep 2026) are not yet checked against the EA and are not used.

**Net effect on the novelty claim:** narrower. Not new: volume size causes ground delay between operators (Pradeep 2023); strategic deconfliction trades risk for delay (Riga 2023); layered vs free airspace for competing operators (Melgosa 2026). Still not found: the same costs computed at metro scale from the companies' own filed Texas layouts, by company, with the layer-gap-vs-reservation effect and wind conformance.
