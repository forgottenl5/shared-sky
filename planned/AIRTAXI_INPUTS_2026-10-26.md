## 3. Flight profiles

### Vertiport obstacle surfaces (FAA EB 105, 21 Sep 2022; EB 105A, 27 Dec 2024)

| Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Primary surface = FATO, horizontal at vertiport elevation. | EB 105 §2.5.1 (NBAA copy https://nbaa.org/wp-content/uploads/aircraft-operations/emerging-technologies/advanced-air-mobility-aam/20220921-FAA-Engineering-Brief-105-Vertiport-Design.pdf); EB 105A §2.6.1 https://www.faa.gov/airports/engineering/engineering_briefs/eb_105a_vertiports | VERIFIED |
| **Approach/departure surface slope 8:1** (H:V). It starts at the FATO edge at FATO width and widens to **500 ft wide at 4,000 ft horizontal** (so it reaches 500 ft above vertiport elevation at 4,000 ft). | EB 105 / 105A | VERIFIED |
| **Transitional surfaces slope 2:1**, extending to 250 ft horizontally from the centerline. | EB 105 / 105A | VERIFIED |
| Paths are aligned with the prevailing wind. Reciprocal paths are preferred; otherwise at least 135° separation. | EB 105 §2.5.2; 105A | VERIFIED |
| Curved paths allowed (105A): S + R ≥ 1,886 ft, R ≥ 886 ft, total centerline ≥ 4,000 ft. Not evaluated under Part 77. | EB 105A §2.6.2 | VERIFIED |
| The FAA says VTOL performance data is limited. Climb/descent gradients are listed as a research gap, so 8:1 is an obstacle-clearance surface and not a measured eVTOL flight path. | EB 105 §II, p.2–3 | VERIFIED |
| Heliport design (AC 150/5390-2D, 5 Jan 2023) uses the same 8:1 / 4,000 ft / 500 ft approach surface family. | AC index page https://www.faa.gov/airports/resources/advisory_circulars/index.cfm/go/document.current/documentnumber/150_5390-2 (values not shown on that page) | AC currency VERIFIED; values UNCERTAIN (from memory) |

**Derived geometry (my arithmetic on the 8:1 surface, measured from the FATO edge and relative to pad elevation):**
- The surface is at 150 ft (46 m) at 1,200 ft (366 m) and at 400 ft (122 m) at 3,200 ft (975 m). It is at 500 ft at 4,000 ft (1,219 m).
- So an aircraft flying exactly on the surface spends horizontal distance 366–975 m inside the 46–122 m drone band.
- Any steeper path shortens that distance. 8:1 is the *shallowest* standard path, but nothing physically stops a pilot from flying below the surface. That makes it a reasonable upper bound, not a hard limit.
- **Elevated or rooftop pads:** add the pad height AGL. A pad 100 ft AGL puts the surface at 150 ft AGL only 400 ft from the FATO edge.
- Width of the affected volume: from FATO width out to 500 ft (152 m) at 4,000 ft, plus 2:1 transitional slopes out to 250 ft (76 m) from centerline.
