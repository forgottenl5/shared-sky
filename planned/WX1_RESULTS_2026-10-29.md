## Result in one line
**In 2025, about 22 % (Houston) and 13 % (DFW) of daytime hours had measurable rain and 12 % / 16 % had 10 m gusts of 40 km/h or more; 4-5 % of daytime hours met a severe-weather label (heavy rain or gusts of 55 km/h or more), and the dataset cannot see thunderstorms at all.** 2 of 6 predictions passed.

## Predictions
- **W1 FAIL** wet share (precipitation >= 0.1 mm): Houston 21.8 % (predicted 8-16 %), DFW 12.7 % (predicted 6-12 %, and DFW was partly informed, see the disclosure). I under-predicted drizzle: 973 Houston hours carry weather code 51 (light drizzle).
- **W2 FAIL** thunderstorm share 0.0 % in both metros, below the predicted 0.3-2.5 %. This is a **data limit**: the archive's weather codes in both files are only 0-3, 51-55, 61-65 and 71-75. Codes for showers (80+) and thunderstorms (95+) never appear, so these files cannot count them. Real thunderstorm hours exist in Texas; this study does not measure them.
- **W3 FAIL** gusty (>= 40 km/h at 10 m) share: Houston 11.8 % (inside 4-14 %), DFW 15.6 % (just above). One of two failed.
- **W4 PASS** hard-stop share 3.9 % (Houston) and 4.6 % (DFW). Because thunderstorms are invisible, this is a lower bound on a label that was meant to include them.
- **W5 PASS** of the 10 windy test hours per metro, 2 are wet (all drizzle or light rain) and none is a thunderstorm hour. The plan-sharing studies' windy hours were mostly dry.
- **W6 FAIL (DFW)** the windy test hours' median gust is 1.70 times the daytime median in Houston (41 vs 24.1 km/h) but 1.41 times in DFW (37.4 vs 26.6), under the registered 1.5.

## Reading
- A model that ignores rain flies on hours that a real operator might stop for: 3.4-4.6 % of daytime hours have 1 mm of rain or more, 1.9-2.3 % have 2.5 mm or more, and 1.6-2.7 % have 10 m gusts of 55 km/h or more. Whether any company stops at those values is not public.
- Weather would remove flights from both the sharing arm and the current-practice arm alike, so it does not change the shape of the headline; it lowers absolute delivery counts by a few percent of hours.
- Rain and strong wind are mostly different hours: the windy test hours were dry, so the plan-sharing cost figures are for dry, windy air.

## Limits
- Open-Meteo archive values are modelled grid-cell data, not airport station observations; its weather codes lack showers and thunderstorms.
- Gusts are at 10 m; drones fly higher, where gusts are probably larger.
- Thresholds are a sweep, not a company rule. No weather limit from Wing, Zipline or Amazon is public except Amazon's "light rain" for the MK30 (no number).
- One year (2025) and one grid cell per metro.
