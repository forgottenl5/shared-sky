## Assessment

The outbound evidence agrees with about 61–69 m. Amazon itself says the Richardson average is 225 ft (69 m), up from about 200 ft (61 m), and the filings model 200–205 ft. The only reports lower than that are residents' estimates (100–170 ft). The return leg is the gap: **no measured, telemetry or company figure for the return leg exists in any public source found.** The only return evidence is the FAA filing bands: 279–377 ft (85–115 m) in TX, FL, DET, KC and the Omaha draft; 325–370 ft (99–113 m) in the Omaha final; and a single modelled 345 ft (105 m). "About 122 m (400 ft) back" goes against every filing; its only source, found on 25 Oct, is a homeowners' association summary of Amazon staff remarks at a May 2025 meeting (GEMINI_OUTSIDE_DATA_CHECK_2026-10-25.md). 400 ft is the stated ceiling ("less than 400 ft AGL"), above the top of every inbound band. The Omaha 61/105 m profile matches the filings and is the best-documented return figure. The return bands run from about 99 m (Omaha final floor) up to 115 m and include 101 m, so a conflict near 101 m on Amazon's return leg is plausible on paper but not shown by any measurement. A further caution: Richardson residents' 150–170 ft readings and the "100 ft" complaints are below even the outbound filing band, which most likely reflects drones still climbing or descending near the hub, or estimation error; the flight phase of those readings is unknown.
