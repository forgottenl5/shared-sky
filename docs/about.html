About Shared Sky

Shared Sky is a personal, unaffiliated simulation project by an anonymous author (forgottenl5), built for learning and as a portfolio piece. It flies the delivery-drone plans that Wing, Zipline and Amazon filed with the FAA for Houston and Dallas–Fort Worth together, and simulates small air-taxi networks for the same metros. It is not affiliated with, or endorsed by, any company or agency named.

What it consists of

  • Two simulation engines written in JavaScript (Node.js, no packages): a delivery-drone engine that builds flights from each company’s filed heights, speeds and hover times and counts where planned paths come close, and an air-taxi network engine (now in its fifth version) with pads, parking stands, batteries, dispatch and queues.
  • 77 studies with 417 registered predictions (316 passed, 97 failed, 4 not scored), each written down and fingerprinted before it ran since 22 October 2026 (exceptions: VALLOC, VALLOC2 and ROBUST were committed with their results, and FLEET’s analysis came after its runs), with Python analysis scripts committed before the runs.
  • Three 3D pages (three.js / WebGL) that replay engine runs; a Lab that runs the delivery-drone engine in your browser; and a findings page.
  • Checks: the air-taxi engine reproduces a published MIT vertiport study; the delivery engine’s growth law was compared with TU Delft’s BlueSky simulator (counts differed); page claims are audited against the study files.

How it was built

The project was built with an AI assistant (Claude). The assistant wrote most of the code, analysis and text under the author’s direction; the author chose the questions, supplied hand-read inputs such as coordinates and drive times, and decided what to publish. Every number on these pages comes from the engines’ runs or from the cited sources, not from the assistant’s memory. The reviews so far were done by AI reviewers: fresh sessions of the same assistant, each given a role. No person outside the project has reviewed the work yet, and nobody at the companies named has commented.

Data

  • FAA environmental assessments for Wing, Zipline and Amazon (heights, speeds, hover times, site counts, daily volumes).
  • US Census Bureau 2020 population and LODES 2022 jobs; FAA UAS facility maps; historical weather from Open-Meteo (CC BY 4.0); buildings © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL).
  • Company announcements and press reports for a few inputs (named on each page); Google Maps “typically” drive times read by hand.

Code, data and contact

The code, registrations and results are in the public repository, github.com/forgottenl5/shared-sky. Questions and corrections: open an issue there. Everything the pages need is served from this site: three.js (MIT licence) and the fonts (SIL Open Font License) are copied in, so the pages make no requests to other servers.
